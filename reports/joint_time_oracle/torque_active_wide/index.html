Joint path-and-time optimization with active torque limits

Scope and qualification. Torque stress is synthetic uniform derating of asymmetric Panda actuator limits; it is not a physical payload experiment. This source inventory contains 30 tasks; moderate/severe deratings are correlated observations of shared source tasks, not independent tasks. Independent checks use aligned Pinocchio RNEA and analytic collision sphere primitives. Added samples surround observed extrema; no continuous-time collision or dynamic certificate is claimed. C is a local optimizer; only individually marked candidates pass sampled physical checks, and no global optimum is claimed.

Methods: A10/A40 native TimeOpt on the frozen cuRobo geometry and modified effort limits (10/40 iterations); B pointwise IPOPT timing on the original geometry; C SLSQP joint path/time with 14/22/34 controls and four starts per resolution; D the same IPOPT timing solver on C's exact quintic geometry. Primary geometry contrast: B versus D.

Research Implications

These local-search results show whether geometry added value after matched fixed-path timing; they do not establish a global minimum.

  • Moderate. B→D median duration change was 0.44% across 27 verified pairs (paired bootstrap 95% CI [0.33%, 0.53%]); positive values mean the changed geometry retimed faster.
  • Severe. B→D median duration change was 3.47% across 29 verified pairs (paired bootstrap 95% CI [2.79%, 3.83%]); positive values mean the changed geometry retimed faster.

Per-task geometry metrics and plots distinguish path length changes from duration. Where D is faster despite a longer joint-space path, shortening the path alone cannot explain the gain. Similar peak constraint utilization indicates a different feasible path can use the same active limits more effectively; the data do not isolate a unique dynamic mechanism.

Only A10/A40 fixed-geometry timing was rerun under derated effort. The geometric planner itself was not rerun with actuator limits, so superiority over a fully torque-aware cuRoboV2-Fast planner is not established.

Research interpretation

Moderate and severe results are summarized separately. The severe regime is the critical test: a positive B→D effect means a changed C geometry retimed by the same fixed-path solver improved on B's original geometry. Gains do not establish that broad geometry search is exhausted. C convergence is incomplete despite multistart, homotopy exploration is limited, and finite sampling and omission of a full planner rerun under derated torque remain material limitations. No global optimality claim is made.
