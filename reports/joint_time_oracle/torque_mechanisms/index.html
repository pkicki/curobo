Interpretation and limits

The severe cohort's B→D paths are generally longer while execution is faster. Across the 20-task decomposition, the median geometric path increase is positive, median gravity RMS change is near zero, and inertial RMS rises in every case. This is consistent with redistributing actuator demands across joints and along the motion, rather than globally reducing torque magnitude. The x′ interval plots expose which joints constrain local path acceleration, but do not establish a unique causal explanation.

A positive adaptive-verified Full-R→D effect means the offline joint optimizer beat the best independently retimed native geometry found within the tested restart budget. A nonpositive effect only shows that this search found a match; it does not imply global optimality. Source tasks are paired across regimes and must not be counted as independent cases.

Sampled and adaptive checks are finite samples; there is no continuous-time certificate. Collision validation uses the configured analytic sphere model. Results are exploratory and limited to frozen Franka scenes and synthetic derating.
